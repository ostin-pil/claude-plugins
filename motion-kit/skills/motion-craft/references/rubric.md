# Judging a motion: the rubric

The judge sees the brief, a contact sheet of the reel, an onion skin, the motion-path report and plot, and, if there is one, the previous take's same material. It does not see the maker's reasoning, pose sheet or hopes: a judge who knows the intent sees it. It scores each check below and quotes its evidence.

## Verdict per check

- **Reads** (2): clearly right on this check; evidence shows it.
- **Weak** (1): present but too small, too late, or inconsistent; say which.
- **Broken** (0): absent or wrong; say what the eye or the numbers show.
- **Can't tell** (blank): the material doesn't show it; say what would.

## The checks

| # | Check | What to look at | Evidence that settles it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Says the brief.** Does the motion read as the brief says (hurried, careful, tired)? | The contact sheet as a sequence | Name the poses that sell or undercut it |
| 2 | **Overlap.** Do the parts start and stop at different times, the mass leading and the extremities following? | Onion skin around the reversals; the probe's lag | Lag of the extremity behind its parent, in frames or poses (0 is broken) |
| 3 | **Arcs.** Do the extremities travel on arcs, without corners or straight back-and-forth lines? | The path plot, with the body's motion taken out: `--relative-to` the limb's own root joint (the shoulder for a hand, the hip for a foot). Relative to the chest, a shoulder riding the breath or a look drifts the path a few pixels a stride, enough to close or flip a thin loop | The probe's sharpest turn and its reversals per cycle |
| 4 | **Spacing.** Does the spacing ease where weight says it should (tight near extremes of a swing, fast in and slow out of a heavy move)? | The path plot's dot spacing; the probe's spacing ratio | Speed at the extremes against speed at the middle |
| 5 | **Asymmetry.** Is it free of twins: left and right the same, forward and back the same, a swing centred where the body wouldn't centre it? | Contact sheet; probe ranges | Forward against back range; left against right |
| 6 | **Body mechanics.** Does the chain make sense: weight over the feet, the chest answering the hips, the head settling? | Contact sheet | Which pose breaks balance, if any |
| 7 | **Timing and cadence.** Does the speed suit the body (no scurrying short legs, no ticking tiny swings)? | Steps per second against leg length (Froude), poses per swing | Fr and distinct poses per sweep |
| 8 | **Clearance.** Does anything pass through the body, clothes or props? | Contact sheet close up; any headless sweep's report | The pose and the part |
| 9 | **Readable at the gameplay camera.** Does the idea survive at the size and distance the player sees it? | A frame at the gameplay camera | Whether the key change is visible there |
| 10 | **Clean loop and blends.** No pop at the cycle's seam or into and out of the clips around it | The frames around the seam and the transitions | The frame of any pop |
| 11 | **Appeal.** Is it pleasant to watch on the hundredth loop? | The reel | One sentence |

## Habits of a good judge

- Score from evidence, and say what evidence would change the score.
- Prefer one concrete next change over a list of ten.
- Separate taste from faults: a clip can be correct and still not what the owner wants; say which kind each remark is.
- When numbers and eyes disagree, report both; the numbers are often measuring the wrong point, and the eye is often habituated.
