# Session 6: three tells with no shape, and the drafting habit behind them

**Date**: 2026-08-25
**Branch**: `feature/session-6-authoring` (worktree `.claude/worktrees/session-6-authoring`)
**Type**: docs

## Context

A review round on a klin PR turned up three writing tells that prose-mint does
not catch. K. asked for them to be written into prose-mint, along with guidance
for agents to compose long text in blocks and merge before publishing.

## The three tells

**One connective doing all the work.** A single contrast pivot, in this case
"rather than", used five times in one PR body as the default joint between
clauses. The give-away is frequency: one is unremarkable, and five means the
relationship between those clauses was defaulted to and never chosen. Every
instance wanted something different once looked at, including two where the
contrast was invented and both halves were simply true.

**A reference nobody can resolve.** The body said "rule 5 fired three times",
naming a numbered item in a policy document the reader has no access to. The
writer had the list in front of them. A reader next week does not.

**A principle restated in place of evidence.** A closing sentence asserting the
paragraph's own claim as a general truth: "deriving a family from a string that
happens to contain a word is the invention this module exists to prevent." It
adds nothing checkable and reads as emphasis. Replaced with the concrete reason,
which is that name-matching fails silently in both directions.

## Why none of them is mechanized

This was the substantive finding, and it is the reason the change is
documentation and not a detector.

The first is a frequency judgment about one phrase. The structural categories
carry a threshold, so the shape exists, but adding a category to
`ALL_CATEGORIES` is not a local decision: `tests/test_source_drift.py` pins that
list to the upstream Untype scanner, and the guard exists because a category
once landed upstream and went unnoticed here for days. Adding one from this side
forks the very parity that test defends. The banlist is prose-mint's own layer
and would be the right venue, except it reports at a count of one, which would
flag a single correct "rather than".

The second and third depend on what a reader already knows. `\brule \d+\b` would
fire on every legitimate numbered reference in a document that defines its
rules, and no regex distinguishes a principle that earns its sentence from one
that pads it.

So all three are written down where a reader will meet them, and the README says
plainly that they are unmechanized and why. Documenting the reason matters more
than the rule: the next person to notice the "rather than" tic will otherwise
reach for a detector and hit the same wall.

## The drafting method

The three tells share a cause worth naming. Drafting a long piece top to bottom
makes each sentence reach for the one before it, and the reaching produces all
three: a connective gets reused because it worked a paragraph ago, a claim gets
restated as a principle because the paragraph felt thin, a reference gets
shortened to a number because it was spelled out earlier.

Writing each paragraph against its own one-line brief, without reading the
neighbours, then concatenating and reading the whole for repeated connectives
and duplicated claims, breaks that chain. A paragraph written alone has to carry
its own weight, and the crutches are visible when they are the only thing
holding it up. It also makes revision cheap, since "rewrite this section" then
addresses a unit instead of a thread running through the whole piece.

Verified on the PR that prompted this: rewriting it block by block removed all
five "rather than" instances, two of which had not been flagged by the reviewer
and were only visible once each paragraph stood alone.

## Also added

The local pre-check for a PR body, which is the command the CI action runs:

```
cat body.md | prose-mint scan --stdin --label "PR #NNN" --strict
```

Worth having as a documented step because a repo may disable `hard-wrap` for
files that wrap at 80 columns by choice while the body is scanned with it
enabled. Discovering that asymmetry from a red build costs a force-push, and it
cost two on klin the same day.

## Still open

- **prose-mint's CLI is not UTF-8-safe on Windows.** The failure above is not
  only a test artefact: `bulk` and `scan` write findings to stdout, and a
  finding for `em-dash` contains an em dash, which cp1252 cannot encode either.
  The tool most likely crashes on Windows on exactly the documents it exists to
  flag. klin's own `cli.py` already carries the fix, `stream.reconfigure(
  encoding="utf-8", errors="replace")`, with a comment about cp1252 mangling
  quoted rule text. Not fixed here because it is unrelated to this session's
  subject and deserves its own change with its own test.
- Mechanizing the connective tell needs either a per-phrase threshold on the
  banlist or a sanctioned way to carry a prose-mint-local structural category
  without failing the upstream parity guard. Either is a design decision, not a
  patch.
